The Round Door Hobbit Study

Light the lamp, pour the tea, and follow one reluctant homebody all the way to a dragon's mountain and back. Read a chapter together, then wander through the subjects below.

How to use this study
i

Read aloud

One chapter a sitting, by lamplight if you can. Little ones can draw while they listen.

ii

Tell it back

Ask each child to retell the chapter in their own words before any questions.

iii

Pick one or two tabs that suit the day. Nobody needs to do everything.

How the storyteller works

A chatty narrator

The narrator often stops to speak straight to the listener, like a grandparent by the fire. Find a moment where this happens and read it in your best storytelling voice.

Songs in the story

Dwarves, elves and goblins all sing. Each song shows something about who is singing. What does a grumbling song tell you that a merry one does not?

Hints of what's coming

Small details, like a bare patch in a dragon's armour, matter much later. Keep a list of clues and check them off as they come true.

There and back again

Bilbo leaves home, is changed by the road, and comes back different. Compare the Bilbo of chapter one with the Bilbo of chapter nineteen.

The moon lab

Waiting for the right moon

In the story, the secret door can only be found on one special evening, when the last moon of autumn and the sun are in the sky together. A thin new crescent hangs low in the west just after sunset, close to where the sun went down. Slide to day 1 or 2 to see that moon.
